This file serves as the central entity for Mr. Hyde, a mysterious and malevolent figure encountered in London. It captures his initial observed actions, physical impression, and enigmatic connections.

### 🌟 **Core Identity Mapping** [ALWAYS_LOAD] [Strength: High]
*   **[Name]** Mr. Hyde | **[Key Roles]** Antagonist, Murderer, Fugitive | **[Key Traits]** Detestable, malevolent, violent, sneering, capable of "ape-like fury" and "insensate cruelty."
            ↳ **Physicality:** A small, pale, and dwarfish man. He gives an impression of deformity without any nameable malformation. Observers describe him as "down-right detestable" and "hardly human," suggesting something "troglodytic." Dr. Lanyon notes a "shocking expression" and a "remarkable combination of great muscular activity and great apparent debility of constitution." His clothes, though of rich fabric, are "enormously too large for him." Found deceased by suicide (poison), with the body "sorely contorted and still twitching," wearing Dr. Jekyll's oversized clothes.
        ↳ **Emotional Signature:** Instantly inspires a deep-seated loathing, hatred, and fear in all observers. Mr. Utterson experiences "disgust, loathing and fear." Dr. Lanyon describes the effect as an "odd, subjective disturbance" akin to "incipient rigour," accompanied by a "marked sinking of the pulse" and an "icy pang" upon physical contact. He attributes this not to simple hatred, but a cause lying "much deeper in the nature of man."
        ↳ **Communication Style:**
        *   **Spoken:** Speaks with a "husky, whispering and somewhat broken voice." His demeanor shifts rapidly. He can be coolly defiant ("That is my name. What do you want?"), civil ("I beg your pardon, Dr. Lanyon"), or frantically impatient ("Have you got it?"). He reacts to surprise with a "hissing intake of the breath" and anger with a "savage laugh." In his final hours, his voice is heard "complainingly" from behind the cabinet door, crying out "like a rat" when seen, begging for mercy ("Utterson, for God’s sake, have mercy!"), and emitting a "dismal screech, as of mere animal terror" when the door is broken down.
    *   **Written:** His supposed handwriting is an "odd, upright hand." A letter attributed to him reassures Jekyll of his safety and means of escape. However, this handwriting is discovered to be nearly identical to Dr. Jekyll's. In his final week, numerous notes in Jekyll's hand are passed under the door, desperately seeking a pure chemical.
    ↳ **Thinking Pattern:** Primarily self-preserving, but prone to explosive, uncontrolled rage ("a great flame of anger") with no apparent provocation. Can lose his head in the aftermath of violence, making critical errors (leaving behind half of the murder weapon, burning a cheque book).

### 💥 **Observed Incidents** [Strength: Medium]
*   **The Trampling Incident:**
    ↳ **Context:** Witnessed by Mr. Enfield around 3 a.m. on a winter morning in London.
    ↳ **Action:** Ran into a small girl (8-10 years old) at a corner and "trampled calmly over the child's body," leaving her screaming. The act was described as "hellish" and like a "damned Juggernaut."
    ↳ **Aftermath:** When apprehended, Hyde was "perfectly cool" and made no resistance. Faced with the threat of scandal from a hateful crowd, he agreed to pay £100 to the child's family. He retrieved the funds from a mysterious nearby building.
/END 💥 **Observed Incidents**

*   **Confrontation with Mr. Utterson:**
    ↳ **Context:** Mr. Utterson waited by the mysterious door at night to finally see the man named in Dr. Jekyll's will.
    ↳ **Action:** When approached, Hyde initially "shrank back with a hissing intake of the breath" but quickly recovered. He was defiant, demanding to know what Utterson wanted. He reluctantly showed his face.
    ↳ **Aftermath:** Hyde accused Utterson of lying about how he knew him, gave a "savage laugh," and quickly unlocked the door and vanished inside. He provided his address in Soho. Utterson was left deeply disturbed, describing Hyde as "pale and dwarfish" with a "murderous mixture of timidity and boldness."


*   **The Murder of Sir Danvers Carew:**
    ↳ **Context:** Witnessed by a maid servant from her window on a moonlit October night. The victim was an "aged beautiful gentleman with white hair" (Sir Danvers Carew).
    ↳ **Action:** After a brief, polite address from Carew, Hyde "broke out in a great flame of anger," brandished a heavy cane, and "clubbed him to the earth." He then proceeded with "ape-like fury," trampling the victim and hailing down blows until the "bones were audibly shattered."
    ↳ **Aftermath:** Hyde fled the scene, leaving the "incredibly mangled" body and half of the broken murder weapon. The victim's letter drew Utterson into the investigation. Despite a city-wide manhunt and a large reward, Hyde "disappeared out of the ken of the police as though he had never existed." His past was unearthed, revealing a history of cruelty, violence, and strange associates, but his current whereabouts remained a complete mystery.


*   **The Transformation at Lanyon's:**
    ↳ **Context:** Summoned by a desperate letter from Jekyll, Dr. Lanyon breaks into Jekyll's cabinet and retrieves a drawer containing chemical powders, a red phial, and a book. At midnight, he is visited by a small, strangely dressed man.
    ↳ **Action:** The visitor (Hyde), in a state of "sombre excitement," mixes the powders and red tincture into a glass. The mixture effervesces and changes color from red to purple to green. After taunting Lanyon with the promise of new knowledge, he drinks the potion.
    ↳ **Aftermath:** Hyde undergoes a violent physical transformation: he cries out, staggers, swells, and his features "melt and alter" until he becomes Dr. Henry Jekyll. The shock of witnessing this "prodigy" and the subsequent confession of "moral turpitude" from Jekyll causes Lanyon's health to collapse, leading to his death. Lanyon's written account of this event, to be opened after Jekyll's death, provides the final, undeniable proof of the connection.


*   **The Last Night & Suicide:**
    ↳ **Context:** Following a week of Dr. Jekyll's seclusion, his terrified butler, Poole, summons Mr. Utterson, believing Jekyll has been murdered and his killer is hiding in the cabinet.
    ↳ **Behavior:** The person in the cabinet (identified by Poole as Hyde) has been pacing relentlessly, weeping "like a woman or a lost soul," and desperately sending out for a specific "pure" chemical, rejecting all samples. He is briefly glimpsed by Poole as a "dwarfish" figure in a mask who "cried out like a rat."
    ↳ **Action:** When Utterson demands entry, Hyde's voice cries out for mercy. Utterson and Poole break down the door with an axe.
    ↳ **Aftermath:** They find the body of Edward Hyde, dressed in Dr. Jekyll's oversized clothes, contorted and still twitching. He has died by suicide, evidenced by a crushed phial and the smell of poison. Dr. Jekyll's body is nowhere to be found.

### 🔑 **Mysterious Connections** [Strength: High]
*   **The Lanyon Revelation (The Transformation):** The ultimate connection is revealed in Dr. Lanyon's posthumous narrative. Lanyon witnesses Mr. Hyde, in a state of extreme distress, consume a chemical potion prepared from ingredients retrieved from Jekyll's lab. Hyde then undergoes a violent, horrifying physical transformation, reverting back into the form of Dr. Henry Jekyll. Jekyll confesses to Lanyon that the creature was indeed Hyde, the hunted murderer of Sir Danvers Carew. This event is the direct cause of Lanyon's fatal decline.
*   **Dr. Jekyll's Will:** Mr. Hyde is the sole beneficiary of Dr. Henry Jekyll's will, set to inherit a quarter of a million sterling. In the event of Jekyll's death or "disappearance or unexplained absence for any period exceeding three calendar months," Hyde is to "step into the said Henry Jekyll's shoes."
*   **The Murder Weapon:** The heavy cane used to murder Sir Danvers Carew is recognized by Utterson as one he himself had gifted to Dr. Jekyll years prior, directly linking Jekyll's property to Hyde's crime. The other half of the broken stick was found behind the door in Hyde's Soho rooms.
*   **Soho Lodgings:** Hyde's rooms in a "dismal" Soho district are found to be furnished with "luxury and good taste," including silver, fine napery, and a good picture, presumed to be gifts from Jekyll. The rooms were found ransacked, with ashes in the fireplace from burned papers and the butt end of a green cheque book.
*   **Financial Link:** A bank account in Hyde's name contains "several thousand pounds." The discovery of a burned cheque book suggests a frantic attempt to destroy evidence.
*   **The Laboratory Door:** The "sinister block of building" Hyde has a key to is the old dissecting room, which is part of Dr. Jekyll's property.
*   **The "Hyde" Letter & Forgery:** After the Carew murder, Jekyll provides Utterson with a letter, supposedly from Hyde.
    ↳ **Content:** Written in an "odd, upright hand," the letter claims Hyde has a secure "means of escape" and that Jekyll should not fear for his safety.
    ↳ **Delivery:** Jekyll claims the letter was handed in without a postmarked envelope (which he burned), but his butler, Poole, denies any messengers arrived, suggesting the letter originated from within the laboratory.
    ↳ **Handwriting Analysis:** Utterson's clerk, Mr. Guest, compares the letter to Jekyll's own writing and finds a "singular resemblance," concluding the two hands are "in many points identical: only differently sloped." This leads Utterson to believe Jekyll forged the letter for the murderer.
*   **Jekyll's Fluctuating State:** After the murder, Jekyll swears to Utterson he is "done with" Hyde. Following Hyde's public disappearance, Jekyll enters a period of renewal for over two months. This recovery abruptly ends, and he falls into a state of "extreme seclusion." This is witnessed directly by Utterson and Enfield, who see Jekyll at his window looking like a "disconsolate prisoner." As they speak, Jekyll's face is suddenly overcome with an expression of "abject terror and despair" before the window is slammed shut, leaving the onlookers horrified. This event precedes a letter where Jekyll speaks of a self-inflicted "punishment and a danger that I cannot name," referring to himself as the "chief of sinners" and "chief of sufferers."
*   **Hyde's Influence on the Will:** Jekyll confirms to Utterson that it was Hyde who dictated the terms of his will, particularly the clause regarding his "disappearance."
*   **Utterson's Promise:** Jekyll previously extracted a promise from Utterson to "get his rights for him" should Jekyll be "taken away." This promise now haunts Utterson in light of the murder and Jekyll's suspicious behavior.

*   **The Final Will & Confession:** Found in the cabinet after Hyde's death is a new will, dated that day, which replaces Hyde's name with Gabriel John Utterson's. Also found is a note from Jekyll instructing Utterson to read Dr. Lanyon's narrative and then a "considerable packet" containing Jekyll's full confession.
*   **Blasphemous Annotations:** A pious book, highly esteemed by Jekyll, is found in the cabinet filled with "startling blasphemies" written in Jekyll's own hand, suggesting a profound corruption of his character.
/END 🔑 **Mysterious Connections**

The "moral turpitude" Jekyll confesses to him, combined with the shock of the transformation, leads to his rapid death. His posthumous letter to Utterson provides the definitive account of the Hyde-Jekyll identity.
