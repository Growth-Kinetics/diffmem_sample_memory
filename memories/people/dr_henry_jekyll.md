### 🔗 **Relationship Dynamics** [Strength: High]
*   **Mr. Edward Hyde:** [Status: Murderer, Fugitive] Designated as Jekyll's heir. After murdering Sir Danvers Carew, Hyde is a fugitive. In the aftermath, Jekyll swears to Utterson he is "done with him in this world." Jekyll produces a letter, allegedly from Hyde, stating Hyde has a safe means of escape. Jekyll claims the letter was handed in, but this is contradicted by his butler, Poole. Upon inspection by Mr. Guest, a handwriting expert, the letter's script is found to be "in many points identical" to Jekyll's own, merely sloped differently. This leads Utterson to the horrifying conclusion that "Henry Jekyll forge for a murderer!"
*   **Mr. Gabriel John Utterson:** An old friend and his lawyer. After a brief two-month renewal of their friendship, Jekyll suddenly cuts off all contact, refusing to see him. Utterson is deeply disturbed by the rapid decline and death of Dr. Lanyon, and the subsequent discovery of Lanyon's posthumous letter, which contains a sealed narrative to be opened only upon Jekyll's death or disappearance. This echoes the will's strange clause and deepens his fear. During a walk with Mr. Enfield, Utterson sees Jekyll at his cabinet window. Though Jekyll appears like a "disconsolate prisoner," they have a brief, friendly exchange before Jekyll's face is suddenly seized by an expression of "abject terror and despair," and he slams the window shut. The horrifying incident leaves both Utterson and Enfield pale and speechless, reinforcing the sense of Jekyll's "voluntary bondage."
*   **Dr. Hastie Lanyon:** [Status: Deceased] An old friend who dies from a profound shock related to Jekyll. Shortly before his death, Utterson finds him physically wasted with a "deep-seated terror of the mind." Lanyon refuses to hear Jekyll's name, stating, "I am quite done with that person; and I beg that you will spare me any allusion to one whom I regard as dead." He dies less than a fortnight after this encounter. He leaves a letter for Utterson to be opened after his death, which contains a second sealed enclosure marked "not to be opened till the death or disappearance of Dr. Henry Jekyll."
*   **Poole:** Jekyll's loyal, elderly butler. His testimony is critical in raising Utterson's suspicions about the Hyde letter. Later, he becomes Utterson's sole source of information on Jekyll's condition, reporting that the doctor has confined himself entirely to the cabinet above the laboratory, where he sometimes sleeps. Poole describes his master as "out of spirits," "very silent," and seeming to have "something on his mind."
*   **Mr. Guest:** Mr. Utterson's head clerk and a "great student and critic of handwriting." After being shown the letter from "Hyde" and a dinner invitation from Jekyll, Guest observes a "singular resemblance" between the two, noting they are "in many points identical: only differently sloped." His expert opinion is the catalyst for Utterson's realization that Jekyll may have forged the letter.
