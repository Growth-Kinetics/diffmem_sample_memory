### 🔗 **Relationship Dynamics** [Strength: High]
*   **Mr. Edward Hyde:** [Status: Deceased (Suicide)] **[True Identity]** The alternate personality of Dr. Jekyll. Jekyll theorized Hyde was smaller and younger because his evil side was "less exercised and much less exhausted" than his virtuous side. He was unique among mankind as "pure evil," which Jekyll believed was the source of the visceral revulsion others felt towards him. Initially a tool for pleasure, Hyde grew in power and malice. His hatred for Jekyll manifested in "ape-like tricks," such as scrawling blasphemies in Jekyll's books, burning his letters, and destroying a portrait of Jekyll's father. Hyde's terror of the gallows was the only thing that forced him to transform back, a dependency he loathed. He was found dead by suicide in Jekyll's cabinet.
*   **Mr. Gabriel John Utterson:** Summoned by a terrified Poole, Utterson investigates the strange occurrences at Jekyll's home. Convinced that Jekyll has been murdered by Hyde, he leads the effort to break down the cabinet door. Inside, he discovers Hyde's body and, to his amazement, a new will that names him, Gabriel John Utterson, as Jekyll's sole beneficiary. He also finds Jekyll's final letter and a sealed confession, which he takes home to uncover the truth.
*   **Dr. Hastie Lanyon:** [Status: Deceased] An old friend who died from the shock of witnessing Mr. Hyde drink a potion and physically transform into Dr. Henry Jekyll. At Jekyll's desperate request, Lanyon broke into the laboratory cabinet to retrieve the transformative chemicals for Hyde. Lanyon documented this horrifying event in a narrative for Utterson to read after his death, providing the first concrete explanation of Jekyll's secret.
*   **Poole:** Jekyll's loyal butler. His terror and conviction that "foul play" has occurred are the catalyst for the final events with Utterson. He provides critical evidence: the changed voice, the lighter footstep, the desperate search for a pure chemical, and the sighting of a masked figure (Hyde). He bravely assists Utterson in breaking down the cabinet door. The instructions he followed to help Lanyon retrieve the chemicals came from a letter written by Hyde (in Jekyll's hand) from a hotel, where Hyde was hiding after an involuntary transformation.
*   **Mr. Guest:** Mr. Utterson's head clerk. His handwriting analysis confirmed that Jekyll likely forged a letter for Hyde, a key piece of the early mystery.

### 🔬 **Scientific & Domestic Context** [Strength: High]


*   **Logistical Preparations for Hyde:** To facilitate his double life, Jekyll made several careful arrangements. He furnished a house in Soho for Hyde, hired a "silent and unscrupulous" housekeeper, and informed his own servants that Hyde was to have full liberty of his main house. To avoid financial ties after an early incident, he opened a separate bank account in Hyde's name and created a unique, back-slanted signature for him.
*   **The Loss of Control & Involuntary Transformations:** The balance of power began to shift irreversibly. The first major warning was waking up one morning in his own bed having transformed into Hyde during his sleep, without taking the potion. Over time, the transformations became spontaneous, with the final one occurring in public at Regent's Park. The difficulty shifted from needing the potion to become Hyde, to desperately needing it (in increasing doses) to turn back into Jekyll. This culminated in him being permanently trapped as Hyde.

### 🧪 **The Transformative Potion** [Strength: High]
*   **Ingredients:** As retrieved by Dr. Lanyon from Jekyll's cabinet, the key components included:
    ↳ **Powders:** A "simple crystalline salt of a white colour," noted by Lanyon as being privately manufactured by Jekyll.
    ↳ **Tincture:** A phial containing a "blood-red liquor," with a pungent smell, believed to contain phosphorus and some volatile ether.
*   **Process:** When a powder was mixed with a few minims of the red tincture, the mixture would effervesce, change color from red to purple to green, and then be ready for consumption.
*   **Logbook:** A notebook found with the chemicals contained a series of dates over many years, recording the experiments. It included notes like "double" and one "total failure!!!", with the entries ceasing about a year before the final events.
/END 🧪 **The Transformative Potion**
*   **The Cabinet & Final Scene:** The cabinet above the laboratory becomes the epicenter of the mystery. After breaking down the door, Utterson and Poole find it strangely peaceful, with a fire glowing and tea things laid out. However, they discover the body of Edward Hyde, a suicide. Dr. Jekyll is nowhere to be found. Key items in the room include a cheval-glass (mirror), various measured piles of a white salt, and a pious book of Jekyll's defaced with handwritten blasphemies.
*   **The Desperate Search for a Drug:** In his final week, trapped as Hyde, Jekyll orchestrated a frantic search for a key ingredient. He sent Poole to multiple chemists with notes, but rejected every new supply as "not pure." In his confession, Jekyll concludes his fate was sealed by an accident of chemistry: "I am now persuaded that my first supply was impure, and that it was that unknown impurity which lent efficacy to the draught." The new, chemically pure salts were useless, which ultimately trapped him in the form of Hyde.
/END 🔬 **Scientific & Domestic Context**

Jekyll created this as a safeguard, so that "if anything befell me in the person of Dr. Jekyll, I could enter on that of Edward Hyde without pecuniary loss." *   **The New Will:** A second will, also in Jekyll's hand, is discovered in the cabinet after Hyde's death. This new version, dated recently, replaces Edward Hyde's name with that of Gabriel John Utterson, making the lawyer the sole beneficiary of Jekyll's estate. Utterson is stunned that Hyde, who was in possession of the cabinet, did not destroy it.
