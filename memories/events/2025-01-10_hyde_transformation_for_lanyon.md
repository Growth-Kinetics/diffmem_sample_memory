### ⏰ Chronology of the Event [Strength: High]
*   **The Request (Jan 9th):** Dr. Lanyon receives an urgent registered letter from Henry Jekyll, pleading for help to avoid the "shipwreck of my reason."
*   **The Errand (Night of Jan 9th):**
    ↳ Lanyon travels to Jekyll's house, meeting the butler, Poole.
    ↳ A locksmith and carpenter are present to force open the door to Jekyll's cabinet.
    ↳ Lanyon retrieves a specific drawer (marked "E") as instructed.
    ↳ He returns with the drawer to his home in Cavendish Square.
*   **The Arrival (Midnight, Jan 10th):** A small, strangely dressed man (Hyde) arrives at Lanyon's door, claiming to be Jekyll's messenger. He displays extreme agitation and fear.
*   **The Preparation:**
    ↳ Hyde, with immense relief, confirms the drawer's contents.
    ↳ He requests a graduated glass and prepares a potion by mixing a "red tincture" with a white crystalline powder.
    ↳ The mixture effervesces and changes color from reddish to purple, then to a watery green.
*   **The Choice:** Hyde offers Lanyon a choice: let him leave with the potion, or stay and witness a "prodigy" that will open "a new province of knowledge." Lanyon, driven by curiosity, chooses to stay.
*   **The Transformation:**
    ↳ Hyde drinks the potion in one gulp.
    ↳ He undergoes a violent physical convulsion: he cries out, reels, swells, his face becomes black, and his features "melt and alter."
    ↳ In his place stands Dr. Henry Jekyll, "pale and shaken."
*   **The Confession:** For the next hour, Jekyll reveals his secret to a horrified Lanyon, including the fact that the "creature" Hyde is the wanted murderer of Sir Danvers Carew.
/END ⏰ Chronology of the Event

### 🔬 Key Components & Ingredients [Strength: Medium]
*   **The Drawer (from Cabinet Press "E"):**
    ↳ **Powders:** Described as a "simple crystalline salt of a white colour," privately manufactured by Jekyll.
    ↳ **Phial:** Contains a "blood-red liquor," pungent, possibly containing phosphorus and a volatile ether.
    ↳ **Paper Book:** A record of experiments spanning many years, with entries ceasing about a year prior. Contains brief remarks like "double" and one emphatic "total failure!!!".
*   **The Potion:** A mixture created by Hyde using a few minims of the red tincture and one of the powders in a graduated glass. It undergoes a visible chemical reaction (effervescence, color change) before being consumed.
/END 🔬 Key Components & Ingredients

### 🎭 Participants & Roles [Strength: Medium]
*   **Dr. Hastie Lanyon:** The narrator and sole witness. Initially skeptical and believing Jekyll to be insane, he follows the instructions out of professional duty. His scientific materialism is shattered by the event, leading to a fatal psychological breakdown.
*   **Mr. Hyde:** The messenger. Described as small, physically repulsive, and wearing oversized clothes. He is in a state of extreme distress and desperation, needing the potion to revert to his Jekyll form. His presence causes a "disgustful curiosity" and physical revulsion in Lanyon.
*   **Dr. Henry Jekyll:** The orchestrator and ultimate subject. He appears after the transformation, "pale and shaken," and proceeds to confess his double life to Lanyon. His letter reveals his desperation and reliance on Lanyon's friendship.
*   **Poole:** Jekyll's butler. Follows Jekyll's written orders to admit Lanyon and assist in breaking into the cabinet.
/END 🎭 Participants & Roles

### 💥 Impact & Aftermath [Strength: High]
*   **On Dr. Lanyon:** The event is described as a "shock" from which he will never recover.
    ↳ **Psychological Trauma:** His "life is shaken to its roots," he suffers from sleeplessness and "the deadliest terror."
    ↳ **Existential Crisis:** The "prodigy" shatters his rational, materialist worldview. He dies "incredulous" despite having witnessed the event.
    ↳ **Moral Horror:** He is sickened by the "moral turpitude" Jekyll unveiled, particularly the knowledge that Hyde is a murderer.
*   **On the Narrative:** This event provides the first concrete explanation for Jekyll's strange behavior and Hyde's existence, delivered posthumously to Mr. Utterson. It is a key piece of evidence that unlocks the mystery.
