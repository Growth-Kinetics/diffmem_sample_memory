# 🧿 scientific_heresy Integration System [Strength: Medium]
This file serves as the central entity for the point of scientific disagreement that caused the estrangement between Dr. Jekyll and Dr. Lanyon, which Lanyon refers to as 'unscientific balderdash'.

/START
### 🔬 Core Definition & Nature [ALWAYS_LOAD] [Strength: High]
*   **[Concept]** A fundamental, professional schism over a scientific principle.
*   **[Origin]** Stems from research or theories pursued by Dr. Henry Jekyll which deviate radically from conventional scientific practice.
*   **[Key Descriptors]**
    ↳ **Lanyon's Term:** "unscientific balderdash," indicating a perception of it as nonsensical and absurd.
    ↳ **Jekyll's Term:** "scientific heresies," a term he adopts (perhaps mockingly) when describing Lanyon's reaction.

    ↳ **Formal Name:** "transcendental medicine," as named by Hyde/Jekyll to Lanyon.
*   **[Revealed Nature]** The "heresy" is a chemical process involving a crystalline salt and a red tincture that creates a transformative potion. This potion allows a user to physically and psychologically change into a different persona (i.e., Dr. Jekyll transforming into Mr. Hyde). It is the practical application of what Hyde/Jekyll calls "transcendental medicine," a field Lanyon had long derided as impossible. The transformation is a violent, physical event described as a "prodigy to stagger the unbelief of Satan."
/END 🔬 Core Definition & Nature

### 🎭 Character Perspectives [Strength: Medium]
*   **Dr. Hastie Lanyon:**
    ↳ **Stance:** Initially one of professional indignation, which transforms into absolute horror and disbelief upon witnessing the heresy's result. He considers Jekyll "insane" upon reading his letter but feels bound by professional duty and friendship to help.
    ↳ **Emotional Signature:** After the revelation, his primary emotions are existential terror and soul-sickness. He describes his life as "shaken to its roots" and is haunted by a "deadliest terror" that leads to his rapid decline and death.
    ↳ **Assessment:** Having witnessed Hyde transform into Jekyll, Lanyon's scientific worldview is shattered. He dies "incredulous," unable to reconcile the "prodigy" he saw with his "narrow and material views." The event proves to be not just a scientific disagreement, but a confrontation with a reality his mind cannot endure.
*   **Mr. Gabriel John Utterson:**
    ↳ **Stance:** Uninformed and dismissive. As a man of "no scientific passions," he underestimates the gravity of the schism.
    ↳ **Assessment:** Initially concludes it is "nothing worse than that," finding relief that the issue is merely a "point of science" and not something more scandalous or criminal.
*   **Dr. Henry Jekyll:**
    ↳ **Stance:** Defensive and dismissive of Lanyon's critique, yet holding a complex, somewhat contradictory view of his old friend.
    ↳ **Assessment of Lanyon:** Calls Lanyon a "hide-bound pedant" and an "ignorant, blatant pedant," expressing he was "never more disappointed in any man."
    ↳ **Self-Description:** Refers to his own controversial work as his "scientific heresies" when recounting Lanyon's objections.
    ↳ **Contradictory Feelings:** Despite his harsh criticism of Lanyon as a "hide-bound pedant," Jekyll's letter to Lanyon affirms his deep and unbroken affection. He writes, "although we may have differed at times on scientific questions, I cannot remember, at least on my side, any break in our affection." This confirms the estrangement was primarily driven by Lanyon, and that Jekyll still considered Lanyon one of his "oldest friends" whom he would trust with his life, honor, and reason.
/END 🎭 Character Perspectives

### 🔗 Impact & Consequences [Strength: High]
*   **Primary Consequence:** The complete estrangement of Dr. Lanyon and Dr. Jekyll. Lanyon states, "I see and I have seen devilish little of the man."

*   **Ultimate Consequence:** Lanyon's death from the shock and horror of witnessing the transformation. The revelation shatters his worldview and his will to live, leading to a rapid physical and mental decline.
*   **Timeline:** The schism occurred "more than ten years" prior to Utterson's inquiry, severing a friendship that dated back to school and college.
*   **Professional Isolation:** Implies that Jekyll's work has isolated him from at least one prominent, mainstream colleague within London's medical community.
