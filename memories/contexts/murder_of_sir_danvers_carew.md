### 📜 **Event Summary & Chronology** [ALWAYS_LOAD] [Strength: High]
*   **[Event]** The brutal, unprovoked murder of Sir Danvers Carew by Mr. Hyde.
*   **[Date]** A night in October, approximately one year after previous events involving Hyde.
*   **[Timeline]**
    ↳ **~11:00 PM:** A maid witnesses Sir Danvers Carew politely accost Mr. Hyde. Having been suppressed for two months, Hyde's evil nature emerges with a "tempest of impatience" and "roaring" fury.
    ↳ **Minutes later:** Hyde clubs Carew to the ground, trampling him with "ape-like fury" and, as Jekyll later confessed, a "transport of glee." The maid faints from horror.
    ↳ **Immediately After:** Struck by terror, Hyde flees to his Soho rooms, destroys papers, and transforms back into a remorseful Dr. Jekyll before the police are called.
    ↳ **~2:00 AM:** The maid recovers and calls the police. The murderer is gone, but the victim's body and part of the weapon remain.
    ↳ **Next Morning:** Mr. Utterson is summoned by police, identifies the victim, and recognizes the murder weapon as a cane he once gave to Dr. Jekyll.
    ↳ **~9:00 AM:** Utterson leads Inspector Newcomen to Hyde's residence in Soho.
    ↳ **Late Afternoon:** Utterson visits Dr. Jekyll, who looks "deathly sick" (a state later revealed to be profound remorse). Jekyll swears he is done with Hyde and gives Utterson a forged letter from Hyde to cover his own crime.
    ↳ **Following the visit:** Utterson questions Poole, who denies any messenger delivered a letter, confirming the letter originated from within the laboratory.
    ↳ **That Evening:** Utterson consults his head clerk, Mr. Guest, a handwriting expert. Guest compares Hyde's letter with an invitation from Jekyll and finds the handwriting to be identical, just differently sloped. Utterson concludes Jekyll forged the letter for Hyde.
/END 📜 **Event Summary & Chronology**

### 👥 **Individuals Involved** [Strength: High]
*   **Victim:** Sir Danvers Carew.
    ↳ **Description:** An "aged beautiful gentleman with white hair," exuding "innocent and old-world kindness" and "well-founded self-content." A client of Mr. Utterson.
*   **Perpetrator:** Mr. Hyde.
    ↳ **Actions:** Responded to a polite greeting with a "tempest of impatience," clubbing and trampling his victim with "insensate cruelty" and a "transport of glee." Fled the scene before police arrived.
    ↳ **State:** Described as carrying on "like a madman." Jekyll's confession reveals this was the result of his evil side being "long caged" and coming out "roaring." After the initial glee, he was struck by terror, fled to Soho to destroy evidence, and transformed back into Jekyll.
*   **Primary Witness:** A maid servant.
    ↳ **Role:** Witnessed the entire attack from her window overlooking the lane. Described as "romantically given," she was initially musing on the peaceful, moonlit night. The horror of the event caused her to faint for several hours.
*   **Key Investigator:** Mr. Utterson.
    ↳ **Involvement:** Summoned via a sealed envelope found on the victim. He identified both Sir Danvers Carew and the broken cane, linking the crime to the circle of Dr. Jekyll. He led the police to Hyde's address.
*   **Official Investigator:** Inspector Newcomen of Scotland Yard.
    ↳ **Role:** The lead police officer on the case. He accompanied Utterson to Soho, searched Hyde's rooms, and declared his intent to "get out the handbills" and wait for Hyde at his bank.

*   **Associated Party:** Dr. Henry Jekyll.
    ↳ **State:** Found "deathly sick" by Utterson. His confession reveals this was not just illness but profound, genuine remorse, having just transformed back from being the murderer. He resolved to suppress Hyde forever.
    ↳ **Actions:** Provides Utterson with a letter allegedly from Hyde. This was a forgery, created by Jekyll to cover his own crime. The distinct handwriting was a pre-planned deception, achieved by "sloping my own hand backward."
*   **Consulted Expert:** Mr. Guest.
    ↳ **Role:** Utterson's head clerk and a student of handwriting.
    ↳ **Involvement:** Analyzes the "Hyde" letter and compares it to a dinner invitation from Dr. Jekyll, discovering the handwriting is identical but for the slope. His expert opinion confirms Jekyll's forgery for Utterson.

### 🔎 **Evidence & Crime Scenes** [Strength: Medium]
*   **Primary Scene: A Lane Near the River**
    ↳ **Atmosphere:** Initially "brilliantly lit by the full moon" on a cloudless night.
    ↳ **Evidence Found:**
        *   The "incredibly mangled" body of Sir Danvers Carew.
        *   A purse and gold watch (ruling out robbery as a primary motive).
        *   A sealed and stamped envelope addressed to Mr. Utterson.
        *   One splintered half of a heavy, tough wooden cane, which was the murder weapon.
*   **Secondary Scene: Mr. Hyde's Rooms in Soho**
    ↳ **Atmosphere:** A "dismal quarter" shrouded in a "great chocolate-coloured pall" of fog, described by Utterson as a "district of some city in a nightmare."
    ↳ **State of Rooms:** Two rooms, furnished with "luxury and good taste," but found "recently and hurriedly ransacked." Clothes with pockets inside out were on the floor, and drawers were open.
    ↳ **Evidence Found:**
        *   A pile of grey ashes on the hearth from burned papers.
        *   The butt end of a green cheque book, partially surviving the fire.
        *   The other half of the broken murder weapon, found behind a door.
        *   A bank account in Hyde's name containing "several thousand pounds."
*   **Key Document: The "Hyde" Letter**
    ↳ **Description:** A note signed by "Edward Hyde" assuring Dr. Jekyll of his safety and means of escape.
    ↳ **Provenance:** Given to Utterson by Jekyll, who claimed it was hand-delivered. This is contradicted by Poole, suggesting it was written inside the laboratory. Jekyll claimed to have burned the envelope.
    ↳ **Forensic Finding:** Handwriting analysis by Mr. Guest reveals the script is identical to Dr. Jekyll's, only sloped differently. Jekyll's confession confirms this was a deliberate technique ("by sloping my own hand backward") he had developed to give his alter-ego a distinct signature, thus proving the letter is a forgery by Jekyll himself.

### ⛓️ **Connections & Implications** [Strength: High]
*   **The Murder Weapon:** The cane was a gift from Mr. Utterson to Henry Jekyll many years prior. Its use by Hyde and its broken state provide a direct, physical link between Jekyll's respectable world and Hyde's violent criminality, confirming Utterson's deepest fears.
*   **Hyde's Disappearance:** Hyde's flight and the ransacking of his rooms suggest a panicked attempt to destroy evidence. The burning of the cheque book is seen by Inspector Newcomen as a crucial mistake, as "money's life to the man."
*   **Difficulty of Identification:** A major obstacle for the police is the lack of a consistent description of Hyde. He has few associates, has never been photographed, and witnesses only agree on a "haunting sense of unexpressed deformity."
*   **Jekyll's Forgery:** The discovery that Jekyll forged a letter for Hyde marks a turning point for Utterson, shifting Jekyll from victim to accomplice. Jekyll's confession deepens this: the forgery was not for a separate person, but a desperate act of self-preservation to cover the crime he himself had just committed as Hyde. The fact that the different handwriting was a pre-meditated part of his dual identity ("sloping my own hand backward") reveals the "profound duplicity" at the core of his life, making his complicity not just a reaction, but a fundamental aspect of his experiment.
