# 🧿 Jekyll's Urgent Letter to Lanyon Integration System [Strength: Low]
This file serves as the central entity for the urgent letter written by Dr. Henry Jekyll to Dr. Hastie Lanyon. It details the letter's desperate appeal, its specific instructions, and the catastrophic events it set in motion.

/START
### 📜 **Core Content & Instructions** [ALWAYS_LOAD] [Strength: High]
*   **[Appeal]** An urgent, formal plea based on old friendship, overriding recent scientific disagreements. Jekyll places his "life, my honour, my reason" in Lanyon's hands, stating "if you fail me to-night, I am lost."
*   **[Task 1: Retrieval]** Lanyon is instructed to immediately postpone all engagements, take a cab to Jekyll's house, and meet the butler, Poole. They are to force the door to Jekyll's cabinet, where Lanyon must go in alone, open the glazed press marked "E," and retrieve the fourth drawer from the top (third from the bottom).
*   **[Task 2: Delivery]** Lanyon must carry this drawer back to his home in Cavendish Square. At midnight, he is to be alone in his consulting room and admit a messenger who will arrive in Jekyll's name, placing the drawer in this person's hands.
*   **[Stakes]** Jekyll warns that neglecting any part of these instructions, however "fantastic," could charge Lanyon's conscience with Jekyll's "death or the shipwreck of my reason."
*   **[Postscript]** Acknowledges the possibility of a postal delay. If the letter arrives the next morning, Lanyon is to perform the errand when convenient and still expect the messenger at midnight, though it may then be "too late."
/END 📜 **Core Content & Instructions**

/START
### 🔗 **Involved Parties & Dynamics** [Strength: Medium]
*   **Sender:** Dr. Henry Jekyll. Writes from a "strange place" while "labouring under a blackness of distress that no fancy can exaggerate." His desperation is palpable, with his "heart sinks and my hand trembles" at the thought of failure.
*   **Recipient:** Dr. Hastie Lanyon. An old school companion and friend. Though he initially suspects Jekyll is "insane," the gravity of the appeal compels him to act, feeling he cannot set it aside "without a grave responsibility."
*   **Facilitator:** Poole. Jekyll's butler, who receives his own registered letter of instruction and arranges for a locksmith and carpenter to assist Lanyon in breaking into the cabinet.
*   **Messenger:** An unnamed man who is to present himself in Jekyll's name. This individual is later revealed to be Mr. Hyde, who arrives at Lanyon's home at midnight to claim the drawer.
/END 🔗 **Involved Parties & Dynamics**

/START
### 📦 **Specified Contents (Drawer E)** [Strength: High]
*   **General:** The drawer is to be taken "with all its contents as they stand." Lanyon examines them upon returning to his home.
*   **[Powders]** A simple crystalline salt of a white color, which Lanyon deduces are of Jekyll's "private manufacture" due to their less-than-perfect preparation.
*   **[Phial]** A vial about half-full of a "blood-red liquor," described as having a highly pungent smell and containing what Lanyon guesses to be "phosphorus and some volatile ether."
*   **[Paper Book]** An ordinary version book containing a series of dates spanning many years. The entries ceased abruptly nearly a year prior. Appended remarks are brief, with the word "double" appearing multiple times and one early entry marked "total failure!!!".
/END 📦 **Specified Contents (Drawer E)**

/START
### 💥 **Immediate Consequences** [Strength: Medium]
*   **The Errand:** Lanyon follows the instructions precisely. He and Poole oversee a locksmith and carpenter who, after two hours' work, force open the cabinet door. Lanyon retrieves the drawer and returns home.
*   **The Encounter:** At midnight, the messenger (Hyde) arrives. After being given the drawer, he prepares a mixture using the phial and one of the powders. The compound changes from red to purple to a watery green.
*   **The Revelation:** The messenger offers Lanyon a choice: let him leave, or witness a "prodigy to stagger the unbelief of Satan." Lanyon chooses to see the end. The messenger drinks the potion and, in a horrifying transformation, turns into Dr. Henry Jekyll before Lanyon's eyes.
*   **Impact on Lanyon:** The sight and Jekyll's subsequent confession "shaken [his] life to its roots." He is afflicted with a "deep-seated terror of the mind" that leads to his rapid decline and death, as he confides in his narrative to Utterson.
/END 💥 **Immediate Consequences**
