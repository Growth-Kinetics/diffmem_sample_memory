# ✉️ hyde's_letter Integration System [Strength: Low]
This file serves as the central entity for a letter signed by Edward Hyde, given to Mr. Utterson by Dr. Jekyll. It is a key piece of evidence in the investigation of Sir Danvers Carew's murder.

### 📜 **Core Document Details** [ALWAYS_LOAD] [Strength: High]
*   **[Name]** Hyde's Letter
*   **[Stated Author]** Signed "Edward Hyde".
*   **[Recipient]** Dr. Henry Jekyll.
*   **[Key Content]** A brief note assuring the recipient (Jekyll) that he need not worry for the writer's (Hyde's) safety, as Hyde has a "means of escape on which he placed a sure dependence." It also acknowledges Jekyll's "thousand generosities" which were "unworthily repaid."
*   **[Physicality]** Written in an "odd, upright hand." The envelope was allegedly burned by Jekyll before he could show it to Utterson.
/END 📜 **Core Document Details**

### 🎭 **Circumstantial Context** [Strength: Medium]
*   **Origin:** Presented to Mr. Utterson by a "deathly sick" Dr. Jekyll in his cabinet, following the news of Sir Danvers Carew's murder.
*   **Jekyll's Stated Motive:** Jekyll claims to be "at a loss" whether to show it to the police and entrusts it to Utterson, citing concern for his "own character" which the "hateful business has rather exposed."
*   **Delivery Method (Disputed):**
    ↳ **Jekyll's Account:** Claims the letter was "handed in" and the envelope, which bore "no postmark," was burned.
    ↳ **Poole's Contradiction:** Jekyll's butler, Poole, is "positive" that no letters were delivered that day except for circulars by post. This implies the letter did not arrive from outside the house.
*   **Utterson's Initial Inference:** The contradiction leads Utterson to suspect the letter "had come by the laboratory door; possibly, indeed, it had been written in the cabinet."
/END 🎭 **Circumstantial Context**

### ✍️ **Forgery Analysis** [Strength: High]
*   **Analyst:** Mr. Guest, Utterson's head clerk and a "great student and critic of handwriting."
*   **Method:** Guest compares the handwriting of Hyde's letter with that of a dinner invitation from Dr. Jekyll.
*   **Conclusion:** Guest identifies a "singular resemblance" between the two documents. He concludes: "the two hands are in many points identical: only differently sloped."
*   **Utterson's Realization:** The analysis leads Utterson to the horrifying conclusion that Jekyll forged the letter for Hyde. He thinks, "Henry Jekyll forge for a murderer!" and his "blood ran cold."
/END ✍️ **Forgery Analysis**

### 💥 **Significance & Impact** [Strength: Medium]
*   **Initial Effect:** The letter's contents initially relieve Utterson, as it seems to frame the Jekyll-Hyde relationship in a less sinister light than he had feared. He "blamed himself for some of his past suspicions."
*   **Revised Status:** Following Guest's analysis, the letter transforms from a potentially mitigating note into damning evidence of a cover-up. It implicates Dr. Jekyll in forgery to protect a known murderer.
*   **Current Disposition:** Locked in Mr. Utterson's safe.
