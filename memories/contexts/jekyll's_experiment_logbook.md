# 📖 Jekyll's Experiment Logbook [Strength: Low]
This file serves as the central entity for the logbook retrieved from Dr. Jekyll's cabinet. It documents the physical object, its contents, and its critical role in the events described by Dr. Lanyon.

### 📖 **Object Profile & Contents** [ALWAYS_LOAD] [Strength: High]
*   **[Type]** Paper Book | **[Description]** An "ordinary version book".
*   **[Contents]** Primarily a series of dates spanning "a period of many years." The entries are noted to have ceased abruptly nearly a year prior to its discovery by Lanyon.
    ↳ **Annotations:** Most dates have no associated text. A few have brief, appended remarks.
    ↳ **Specific Entries:**
        *   `"double"`: This single word appears "perhaps six times in a total of several hundred entries."
        *   `"total failure!!!"`: This phrase appears once, "very early in the list," and is followed by several exclamation marks.
/END 📖 **Object Profile & Contents**

### 🗄️ **Provenance & Retrieval** [Strength: Medium]
*   **[Original Location]** Dr. Jekyll's private cabinet. Specifically stored in the "glazed press (letter E) on the left hand," within "the fourth drawer from the top" (or "the third from the bottom").
*   **[Associated Items]** The drawer also contained "some powders" (a white crystalline salt) and a "phial" (containing a blood-red, pungent liquor). The logbook, powders, and phial constitute a single, critical package.
*   **[Retrieval Event]** Retrieved by Dr. Hastie Lanyon on the night of January 9th, following urgent, registered instructions from Dr. Jekyll. Lanyon, accompanied by Jekyll's butler (Poole), a locksmith, and a carpenter, forced the cabinet door to access the drawer. The drawer and its entire contents were then taken back to Lanyon's home in Cavendish Square.
/END 🗄️ **Provenance & Retrieval**

### 🔬 **Inferred Purpose & Significance** [Strength: Medium]
*   **[Apparent Purpose]** A record of a "series of experiments." Dr. Lanyon initially assesses these experiments as having led to "no end of practical usefulness," viewing the book with curiosity but finding it told him "little that was definite."
*   **[Actual Significance]** Despite Lanyon's assessment, the book is of "capital importance." Jekyll's letter states that his "life, my honour, my reason" depend on its successful retrieval and delivery to a designated messenger. Its contents, when combined with the other items in the drawer, are essential for the transformation process. The abrupt end to the entries suggests a critical turning point or conclusion in Jekyll's experimental work.
/END 🔬 **Inferred Purpose & Significance**

### 🔗 **Associated Entities & Events** [Strength: Medium]
*   **Dr. Henry Jekyll:** The author and owner of the logbook. The book is the record of his secret scientific investigations. His desperate need for it indicates it is a vital component of his work, not merely a historical record.
*   **Dr. Hastie Lanyon:** The retriever. Following Jekyll's instructions, he secured the book and held it for the messenger. He examined its contents, providing the only known description of the logbook. The events surrounding the book's delivery lead directly to the "shock" that causes his death.
*   **Mr. Hyde:** The intended recipient. He arrives at Lanyon's house as Jekyll's "messenger," in a state of "sombre excitement," and desperately demands the drawer containing the book and chemicals. The items allow him to transform back into Henry Jekyll in front of a horrified Lanyon.
/END 🔗 **Associated Entities & Events**
