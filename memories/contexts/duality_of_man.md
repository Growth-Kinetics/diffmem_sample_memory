# 🧿 duality_of_man Integration System [Strength: Low]
This file serves as the central entity for the philosophical concept of the 'Duality of Man' as articulated and experienced by Dr. Henry Jekyll. It maps the core theory, its origins, and its catastrophic application.

/START
### 🧬 **Core Concept Definition** [ALWAYS_LOAD] [Strength: High]
*   **[Central Tenet]** "that man is not truly one, but truly two." A theory positing that human nature is a compound of two distinct, struggling entities: one representing good, the other evil.
    ↳ **The Two Natures:** Described as "provinces of good and ill which divide and compound man’s dual nature." They are "polar twins" bound together in the "agonised womb of consciousness."
    ↳ **Hypothesized Goal:** To house each nature in a "separate identity." This would relieve life of all that is "unbearable," allowing the "unjust" to act without remorse and the "just" to proceed without being "exposed to disgrace and penitence by the hands of this extraneous evil."
    ↳ **Extended Postulate:** Jekyll hazards a guess that man may ultimately be a "mere polity of multifarious, incongruous and independent denizens," suggesting the duality is only the first observable layer.
/END 🧬 **Core Concept Definition**

### 🧠 **Philosophical & Psychological Origins** [Strength: Medium]
*   **[Primary Driver]** A "profound duplicity of life" stemming from Dr. Jekyll's need to conceal his "pleasures" and "impatient gaiety" to maintain a "more than commonly grave countenance before the public."
    ↳ **Internal Conflict:** This concealment, born from "high views" and "exacting aspirations," was accompanied by an "almost morbid sense of shame."
    ↳ **Self-Perception:** Jekyll did not consider himself a hypocrite, but a "profound double-dealer," because "both sides of me were in dead earnest." The side that "plunged in shame" was as genuine as the side that "laboured...at the furtherance of knowledge."
    ↳ **The Dream:** The idea of separating his two natures became a "beloved daydream" long before his scientific studies suggested it was a physical possibility.
/END 🧠 **Philosophical & Psychological Origins**

### 🧪 **Scientific Application & The Potion** [Strength: Medium]
*   **[Field of Study]** Jekyll's scientific direction led "wholly towards the mystic and the transcendental," which shed light on his internal "perennial war."
*   **[The Mechanism]** A compounded drug, or "tincture," designed to "shake and pluck back that fleshly vestment." The efficacy of the potion was later discovered to be dependent on an "unknown impurity" in the initial batch of a specific "salt."
    ↳ **Drug's Action:** The potion has "no discriminating action; it was neither diabolical nor divine." It merely "shook the doors of the prisonhouse of my disposition," allowing the entity within—in this case, the long-suppressed evil—to "run forth."
    ↳ **The Transformation:** A physically agonizing process involving "racking pangs," "a grinding in the bones, deadly nausea, and a horror of the spirit." The result is the substitution of a "second form and countenance."
/END 🧪 **Scientific Application & The Potion**

### 🎭 **The Resulting Entities & Their Relationship** [Strength: High]
*   **Dr. Henry Jekyll (The Composite):** The original self, described as an "incongruous compound" of good and evil. Even after creating Hyde, Jekyll remains a composite, not pure good. He has a "father's interest" in Hyde but is also filled with "abhorrence" and eventually a "vital instinct" of hatred for the "inorganic" creature caged in his flesh.
*   **Mr. Edward Hyde (The Pure Evil):** The physical embodiment of Jekyll's evil side. He is "smaller, slighter and younger" because that side of Jekyll's nature had been "much less exercised." He is described as "pure evil," a being of "causeless hatreds" who is "indifferent to Jekyll, or but remembered him as the mountain bandit remembers the cavern in which he conceals himself."
*   **[The Struggle]** Initially, Jekyll controls the transformations. However, as Hyde is exercised, he grows in stature and power. The balance is "permanently overthrown," and Jekyll begins transforming into Hyde involuntarily, especially during sleep. The hatred becomes mutual: Hyde resents Jekyll's "despondency" and plays "ape-like tricks" on him, while Jekyll is consumed by the "horror of being Hyde."
/END 🎭 **The Resulting Entities & Their Relationship**
