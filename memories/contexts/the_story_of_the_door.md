# 🧿 The Story of the Door Integration System [Strength: High]
This file serves as the central entity for the narrative told by Mr. Enfield, which introduced the mystery of Mr. Hyde. The events described here—the sinister door, the trampled child, the cheque, and the incident at the window—are now fully contextualized by Dr. Jekyll's final confession, which reveals Hyde was a physical manifestation of Jekyll's own evil nature, created via a chemical potion.

/START
### 📖 **Narrative Summary** [ALWAYS_LOAD] [Strength: Medium]
*   **[Narrator]** Mr. Richard Enfield.
*   **[Audience]** Mr. Gabriel John Utterson.
*   **[Core Event]** In the early hours of a winter morning, a small, detestable man (Mr. Hyde) calmly tramples a young girl and is subsequently cornered by Enfield and a crowd.
*   **[Resolution Explained]** To avoid scandal, Hyde is compelled to pay £100. Jekyll's confession reveals Hyde used his key to the lab and forced the payment from Jekyll to escape. This was not blackmail.
*   **[Key Anomaly Explained]** The cheque was genuine and signed by Dr. Jekyll. This was not due to blackmail, but because Hyde was Jekyll's own creation and needed the funds to escape the crowd.
*   **[Subsequent Event: Incident at the Window]** On a later walk, Enfield and Utterson see Dr. Jekyll at a window. Jekyll's confession explains his appearance as a "disconsolate prisoner" was due to his struggle against Hyde. The expression of "abject terror and despair" was the beginning of an involuntary transformation into Hyde, forcing him to slam the window shut.
/END 📖 **Narrative Summary**

### 📍 **Key Locations & Objects** [Strength: Medium]
*   **The Door / Building:**
    ↳ **Location:** A bystreet in a busy quarter of London.
    ↳ **Identity:** The back entrance to Dr. Jekyll's laboratory. Jekyll's confession reveals it was the portal through which he, as Hyde, could "spring headlong into the sea of liberty" and escape back into his respectable life.
    ↳ **Appearance:** A "sinister block of building" two storeys high, with no windows on the ground floor. The door itself is "blistered and distained."
    ↳ **Condition:** Bears marks of "prolonged and sordid negligence," reflecting the nature of the activities associated with it.
    ↳ **Nickname:** "Black Mail House" (coined by Enfield, based on the incorrect hypothesis of blackmail).
*   **The Cheque:**
    ↳ **Value:** For the balance of a £100 payment (after £10 in gold).
    ↳ **Signatory:** **Dr. Henry Jekyll**.
    ↳ **Authenticity:** Confirmed as genuine. Jekyll's confession clarifies that Hyde, after trampling the child, entered the laboratory and compelled the payment from Jekyll to avoid capture. The mystery of *why* is solved: it was an act of self-preservation for their shared existence.
/END 📍 **Key Locations & Objects**

### 👥 **Characters Involved** [Strength: High]
*   **[Antagonist] Mr. Hyde:**
    ↳ **True Nature:** The pure evil side of Dr. Henry Jekyll, physically separated and given its own form through a chemical potion. He is smaller and younger than Jekyll because Jekyll's evil side was less exercised than his good side.
    ↳ **Key Action:** "Trampled calmly over the child's body." Jekyll's confession confirms this event, noting it was an act of cruelty that briefly endangered Hyde.
    ↳ **Demeanor:** Exhibits a "murderous mixture of timidity and boldness" and a complete lack of conscience.
    ↳ **Physicality & Effect on Others:** Described as "pale and dwarfish" with an "impression of deformity without any nameable malformation." Jekyll theorizes the universal revulsion he inspires is because all other humans are a mix of good and evil, whereas Hyde is uniquely "pure evil."
*   **[Narrator] Mr. Richard Enfield:**
    ↳ **Role:** Primary witness who collared Hyde and negotiated the payment. He provides the initial narrative that sparks Utterson's investigation.
    ↳ **Later Action:** Witnesses the "Incident at the Window" with Utterson, an event now understood to be an involuntary Jekyll-to-Hyde transformation.
*   **[Observer] The Doctor ("Sawbones"):**
    ↳ **Role:** Attended to the trampled child. His extreme reaction is an early example of the universal revulsion Hyde inspires.
*   **[Key Figure / Progenitor] Dr. Henry Jekyll:**
    ↳ **Core Conflict:** Lived a life of "profound duplicity," concealing his shameful pleasures to maintain a respectable public facade. This internal struggle between his good and evil sides led to his scientific quest to separate them.
    ↳ **Relationship with Hyde:** Jekyll is not Hyde's benefactor; he **is** Hyde. Using a potion, he transformed his body into a form that embodied his "lower elements," allowing him to indulge in vice without risking his reputation. The will and orders to his staff were practical measures to protect this dual existence.
    ↳ **Relationship with Lanyon:** The "unscientific balderdash" Lanyon rejected was Jekyll's "transcendental" research into the duality of man, which ultimately led to the creation of Hyde.
    ↳ **Deteriorating State:** Jekyll's initial control over the transformation eroded over time. The "Incident at the Window" was an involuntary transformation. Eventually, he began changing spontaneously, becoming trapped in Hyde's form, leading to his final confession and demise.
/END 👥 **Characters Involved**

### 🧩 **Resolution of the Central Mystery** [Strength: High]
Dr. Jekyll's final confession resolves all mysteries presented in the initial narrative. The central conflict was internal to Jekyll, not an external one like blackmail.
*   **The Nature of Hyde (Solved):** Hyde is not a separate person but the physical embodiment of Dr. Jekyll's evil nature, released by a chemical potion. The universal revulsion he causes is theorized by Jekyll to be a reaction to a being of "pure evil" in a world where everyone else is a mix of good and evil.
*   **The Jekyll-Hyde Connection (Solved):** They are two forms of the same person. Jekyll created Hyde to indulge his vices without consequence to his reputation. The will, the key, and the authority over staff were all safeguards for this dual existence.
*   **Blackmail Hypothesis (Debunked):** The confession proves this theory was incorrect. Jekyll was a willing participant, not a victim. He created Hyde to serve his own desires for a "sea of liberty" from moral restraint.
*   **The Trampling Incident & Cheque (Explained):** Jekyll's confession confirms this event. Hyde, cornered after trampling the child, used his key to the laboratory and forced the payment from Jekyll to escape the situation.
*   **The Incident at the Window (Explained):** This was an involuntary transformation. By this point, Jekyll was losing control, and the change into Hyde began against his will, causing the look of "abject terror and despair" before he slammed the window.
/END 🧩 **Resolution of the Central Mystery**
