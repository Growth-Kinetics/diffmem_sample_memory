This document, which Utterson refused to help draft, is a source of professional and personal offense. He now suspects Jekyll is in "deep waters," possibly being blackmailed for a "ghost of some old sin," and resolves to intervene if Jekyll will allow it.

The man is described as "pale and dwarfish," giving an "impression of deformity without any nameable malformation," with a "displeasing smile" and a "husky, whispering" voice. The encounter provoked an "unknown disgust, loathing and fear" in Utterson, who perceives him as "hardly human" and "troglodytic," seeing "Satan's signature upon a face." Hyde has a key to Jekyll's laboratory entrance and, according to the butler Poole, all of Jekyll's staff have orders to obey him. Hyde provided an address in Soho.

Lanyon has not seen Jekyll for over ten years due to a professional falling-out over what he calls Jekyll's "unscientific balderdash." He has never met or heard of Mr. Hyde.
    ↳ **Poole:** Dr. Jekyll's well-dressed, elderly butler. He confirms to Utterson that Hyde has a key, has full authority over the household staff ("we have all orders to obey him"), and primarily uses the laboratory entrance, never dining in the main house.
