*   **Known Associates:**
    ↳ **Mr. Richard Enfield:** A distant kinsman and close friend. Their bond is a "growth of time," characterized by quiet, regular Sunday walks which they both treasure above business or pleasure. Their friendship is a puzzle to others, as they often walk in comfortable silence.
    ↳ **Dr. Henry Jekyll:** [Status: Complicitous? | Promise Under Strain] The connection between Jekyll and Hyde has become deeply incriminating. The weapon used by Hyde to murder Sir Danvers Carew was a cane Utterson had gifted to Jekyll. This fact horrifies Utterson and implicates Jekyll in the crime, at least by association. Hyde's luxurious rooms in Soho, filled with fine art, are also presumed to be funded by Jekyll. Jekyll's previous reassurances that he could be "rid of Mr. Hyde" at any moment now seem either a deliberate lie or a tragic miscalculation. The promise Utterson made to protect Hyde's rights now weighs on him as a promise to aid a known, brutal murderer.
    ↳ **Mr. Hyde:** [Status: Known Murderer & Fugitive] Hyde has brutally murdered Sir Danvers Carew in the street with "ape-like fury," clubbing him to death with a heavy cane. The cane is a critical piece of evidence, as it was a gift from Utterson to Dr. Jekyll years prior. Following the murder, Hyde fled to his lodgings in a "dismal quarter of Soho," where he ransacked the rooms and burned papers before disappearing. The police, led there by Utterson, found the other half of the murder weapon and a burned cheque book. Despite having several thousand pounds in the bank, he is now a fugitive with no known family or photograph, described only by a "haunting sense of unexpressed deformity." Utterson's promise to protect Hyde's rights is now catastrophically complicated.
    ↳ **Dr. Hastie Lanyon:** An old friend of both Utterson and Jekyll from school and college. Described as a "hearty, healthy, dapper, red-faced gentleman." Lanyon has not seen Jekyll for over ten years due to a professional falling-out over what he calls Jekyll's "unscientific balderdash." Jekyll reciprocates the sentiment, dismissing Lanyon as a "hide-bound pedant" in whom he was "never more disappointed." Lanyon has never met or heard of Mr. Hyde.
    ↳ **Poole:** Dr. Jekyll's well-dressed, elderly butler. He confirms to Utterson that Hyde has a key, has full authority over the household staff ("we have all orders to obey him"), and primarily uses the laboratory entrance, never dining in the main house.
    ↳ **Sir Danvers Carew:** [Status: Deceased] An aged, white-haired, and well-regarded gentleman, murdered by Mr. Hyde. Utterson identified his body at the police station. An envelope addressed to Utterson was found on his person, suggesting a professional or personal connection, and leading the police to involve Utterson in the case.
    ↳ **Inspector Newcomen:** [Status: Investigating] An officer from Scotland Yard investigating the murder of Sir Danvers Carew. Utterson assists him by identifying the victim, recognizing the murder weapon as belonging to Jekyll, and leading him to Hyde's lodgings in Soho.
